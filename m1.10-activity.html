🏭 Workplace Activity

Match the Tool
to the Task

Think like someone on the shop floor. Sort real manufacturing tasks into the right category — and decide where AI helps and where humans lead.

📚 Step 1 — Know Your Categories
🔧
Diagnostics
Machine health, sensors, vibration patterns, early signs of wear — tasks involving data streams over time.
🔍
Quality Control
Checking products for defects, inconsistencies, or missing parts — scanning every item on the line.
🧠
Human Judgment
Decisions that need context, experience, and accountability — safety calls, trade-offs, conflicting signals.
🎯 Step 2 — Match Each Task
Your turn. For each task below, choose the category that fits best. Think about whether it should be done by AI — or whether it needs human judgment and accountability.
💬 Step 3 — Something to Remember
🔄

These categories can overlap — and that's on purpose.

In real factories, AI might support a task, but a human still guides the final call. A machine can flag a defect — but a person decides if it's critical or acceptable. The skill you're building is knowing where AI fits, where it helps, and where human judgment is non-negotiable.

💡 Step 4 — Reflect
💡 Think of one task you do at school, work, or home. Would you trust an AI to handle it — or would you want a human involved? Why?

The Big Idea

AI is a powerful tool on the shop floor — but it's not the decision-maker. Humans set the priorities, interpret the context, and own the outcome. Your judgment is what makes AI useful.
